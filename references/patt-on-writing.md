# Yale Patt on writing

Yale Patt is a professor of electrical and computer engineering at the University of Texas at Austin, known for his work on computer architecture and for teaching introductory computing. His short page "On writing" (https://users.ece.utexas.edu/~patt/writing/) lists the rules he gives students for technical writing. This file summarizes them in our own words; read the original for his phrasing and humor.

His starting point is that what you know is of little use if you cannot express it, and that many students were taught writing "rules" in school that they now need to unlearn. His advice:

1. **Write for the reader.** In his words, "Technical writing is for the benefit of the reader, not the writer." Before writing, think about what your readers already know and what they will bring to the text. Keeping them in mind the whole time gives them the best chance of understanding you. This is the rule behind Rule 1 of this skill (give the reader the context).

2. **Short sentences are better than long ones.**

3. **Small words are better than big ones.** He jokes that the only five-syllable word he uses is "delicatessen."

4. **The verb is the most important word in the sentence.** Choose it with care, and make it carry the action.

5. **Do not invent verbs by adding "-ize" to a noun.** English already has plenty of good verbs.

6. **Active voice is better than passive.**

7. **Ending a sentence with a preposition is acceptable** if the alternative is an awkward sentence. Better still, rework the sentence so it is neither awkward nor ends in a preposition.

8. **Using the same word twice in a paragraph is fine.** Do not reach for a thesaurus to find a near-synonym for the second use. A different word makes the reader wonder whether you mean a different thing.

9. **Spelling mistakes are not acceptable.** Keep a dictionary nearby. If a word is not in a pocket dictionary, you probably should not be using it. A spell checker will not catch a correctly spelled wrong word.
