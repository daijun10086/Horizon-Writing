# Discouraged words for clear-writing
#
# These words are not banned. They are strongly discouraged because AI writing
# overuses them, usually as jargon, metaphor, or self-praise. Use one only when
# it is the most precise word in its plain, literal meaning.
#
# How to edit this file (anyone in the lab can add words):
#   - "# group: <name>" starts a group. "# why:" and "# instead:" explain the
#     group; the checker prints them next to each match, so keep them short.
#   - Each other non-empty line is one entry:
#         form, form, form | except: phrase, phrase
#     Separate the forms of one word with commas. The checker also catches
#     regular endings (-s, -es, -ed, -ing, -ly), so list only irregular forms
#     such as "paid" or "won".
#   - "except:" lists phrases where the word is the normal technical term, so
#     the checker skips it there (for example "race condition").
#   - Lines starting with "#" are comments.

# group: self-vouching
# why: These words ask the reader to trust the writer instead of giving a reason to. Real candor shows in the content.
# instead: Delete the word, or give the evidence that makes the claim believable.
honest, honestly, honesty
genuine, genuinely
legitimate, legitimately

# group: borrowed rigor
# why: These words dress ordinary work up as an audit, a court case, or a formal experiment, so the reader expects more than was done.
# instead: Say what you actually did: "I read the three callers of parse()", not "I audited parse()".
audited, audit
certify, certified, certifies
uncertified
forensic, forensically
provenance
hypothesis, hypotheses
testable
refute, refuted
facts
confirmation
foundational
guarantee, guaranteed, guarantees
qualify, qualifies, qualified
settle, settled, settles

# group: money and games
# why: Betting, buying, and winning metaphors hide the literal cost or benefit behind a figure of speech.
# instead: State the literal cost or benefit with numbers: "this uses 2 GB more memory and halves the run time".
bet, bets, betting
budget, budgets | except: power budget, area budget, memory budget, grant budget
pay, paid, pays, payoff, pays off | except: pay attention
trade, trades, trade-off, trade-offs, tradeoff, tradeoffs
economics
rental
wholesale
win, wins, won, winning, winner
collect a prize, collect the prize, collect the win, collect the reward, collect a reward
race, races | except: race condition, race conditions, data race, data races, race detector, race-free
stacking the deck, stack the deck

# group: physical and engineering metaphors
# why: These borrow a concrete image (a beam, a gate, a blade) for an abstract relationship, and the reader has to decode it.
# instead: Name the actual relationship: "the rest of the proof depends on this lemma", not "this lemma is load-bearing".
load-bearing, load bearing
gate, gates, gated, gating | except: logic gate, logic gates, AND gate, OR gate, NAND gate, NOR gate, XOR gate, gate delay, gate count, gate-level, clock gating, gated clock, gate oxide
screen, screens, screened, screening | except: screen reader, screen readers, on screen, on the screen, screen size, full screen, touch screen, splash screen, login screen, home screen, lock screen
envelope, envelopes
re-arming, re-arm, rearm, rearming
onion-peeling, peeling the onion, peel the onion, peeling back the onion
sharpen, sharpens, sharpened
line in the sand, draw a line in the sand
signal, signals, signaled, signalled | except: signal handler, signal handlers, signal handling, signal processing, clock signal, control signal, control signals, signal integrity, digital signal, analog signal

# group: moral and dramatic words
# why: These words add judgment or drama (purity, punishment, surprise) to technical content that should stay neutral.
# instead: Describe what happened and what it caused, and let the reader judge.
pure, purely | except: pure function, pure functions, purely functional
purity
impure
punish, punishes, punished, punishing
discipline, disciplined
damage, damages, damaged, damaging
hazard, hazards | except: data hazard, data hazards, control hazard, control hazards, structural hazard, structural hazards, RAW hazard, WAR hazard, WAW hazard
surprise, surprising, surprisingly
curiosity
tolerable
lost | except: lost packets, lost updates, lost wakeup, lost write, lost writes

# group: product talk
# why: Product and marketing words make research or engineering work sound like a launch.
# instead: Say what exists now and where: "the fix is merged into main", "the tool is on GitHub".
ship, ships, shipped, shipping

# why: These words appear far more often in AI text than in human writing, usually where a plain word would do. This group was added by the skill author, not taken from the lab's original list; the lab can remove it.

# group: needless phrases (from Strunk)
# why: The Elements of Style, Rule 13 and Chapter V: these phrases add words without adding meaning.
# instead: "the fact that he failed" -> "his failure"; "as to whether" -> "whether"; "one of the most" -> say which and why.
the fact that
as to whether
one of the most
along these lines, along the lines of
literally
